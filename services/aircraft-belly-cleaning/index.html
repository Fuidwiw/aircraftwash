Aircraft Belly Cleaning and Degreasing

Aircraft belly surfaces can collect oil, grease, exhaust residue, and dirt that differ significantly from the lighter contamination found on upper exterior surfaces. Belly cleaning is therefore quoted according to visible condition, accessibility, and the work requested.

The service may be added to a wash or exterior detail, or discussed separately when lower-surface accumulation is the main concern.

Common Belly-Surface Buildup

The requested scope can involve combinations of oil film, grease, exhaust residue, road-like dirt, and other exterior accumulation. The amount, location, and how long it has been present can change the time and handling required.

Oil and grease

Concentrated residue may require more careful product dwell and hand work than a routine exterior wipe-down.

Exhaust residue

Residue patterns can vary across aircraft and use conditions. Cleaning addresses accessible exterior appearance only.

Dirt accumulation

Lower surfaces may hold heavier soil than upper surfaces, especially when routine appearance cleaning has been delayed.

Mixed condition

A belly can contain several kinds of buildup at once, which is why a useful condition description matters when requesting a quote.

Adding Belly Cleaning to a Wash

Belly cleaning is listed as an add-on in the current pricing structure and is also included in the described full exterior detail package. Combining it with wet aircraft washing can coordinate upper- and lower-surface cleaning in one appointment when the work area permits the selected process.

Monthly appearance maintenance also offers an optional belly-degrease add-on. The add-on price does not eliminate condition-based scope review.

When Separate Scoping Helps

If lower-surface buildup is concentrated or significantly heavier than the rest of the aircraft, describe it separately instead of assuming it is part of a routine wash. Photos may help with initial discussion, but the website does not request sensitive aircraft or owner information.

Pricing may vary based on condition, location, travel distance, access requirements, and requested work. Review the supported aircraft-category guidance before requesting a quote.

Aircraft Access and Positioning

The aircraft must be positioned where the allowed work can be completed safely and where the relevant belly surfaces are reasonably accessible. The customer is responsible for arranging aircraft movement, keys, ramp or hangar access, gate or security access, and facility permission when required.

Before the appointment: Explain any access limitations, areas that cannot be reached, known leaks or active maintenance concerns, sensitive finishes, recent work, and manufacturer restrictions that may affect cleaning.

Ozark Aircraft Wash brings its own cleaning equipment, RealClean Aviation Products selected for aircraft cleaning and appearance care, and its own water supply. No claim is made that one product is suitable for every belly surface or finish.

Appearance Cleaning, Not Maintenance or Inspection

Aircraft belly cleaning is exterior appearance care. It does not diagnose the source of oil or residue, detect corrosion, inspect components, establish airworthiness, repair leaks, or replace maintenance. If the owner is aware of an active mechanical concern, that information should be addressed through the appropriate maintenance channel before cleaning is scheduled.

The cleaning scope also does not promise complete removal of every mark or long-standing residue. Condition and surface suitability determine what can be addressed responsibly.

Belly-Cleaning Questions

Is belly cleaning included with every wash?

No. It may be separately added or included in a package whose visible description names belly degreasing. Confirm the requested scope when quoting.

Why is aircraft condition important?

The type, concentration, accessibility, and amount of oil, grease, exhaust residue, and dirt affect the work required.

Does cleaning identify a mechanical problem?

No. The service is appearance cleaning and does not include diagnosis, inspection, repair, corrosion detection, or airworthiness conclusions.
