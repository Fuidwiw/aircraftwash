How a Service Recommendation Is Made

A service name alone does not determine the correct cleaning approach. Before quoting, Ozark Aircraft Wash needs a practical description of the aircraft category, general condition, contamination, proposed work location, and requested outcome.

Light routine contamination may fit a waterless process. Heavier buildup or contamination that should not be handled with repeated wiping may call for a wet wash where water use and runoff are permitted. Belly residue and interior condition can change the scope even when they are combined with exterior work.

Owners should also disclose special coatings, finishes, sensitive surfaces, and known manufacturer restrictions. No product or method is represented as universally suitable for every aircraft surface.

How Mobile Service Works

  1. Describe the request. Provide the aircraft category, general condition, proposed location, service requested, and preferred timing.
  2. Confirm the work area. The customer confirms that washing or appearance work is allowed and arranges ramp, gate, hangar, aircraft, and security access when required.
  3. Coordinate travel and scheduling. At least 24 hours of advance notice is preferred. Availability may vary with travel, weather, access, and existing commitments.
  4. Review the aircraft on arrival. Condition and disclosed surface restrictions help determine whether the requested method remains appropriate.

Access notice: Customers are responsible for arranging aircraft and facility access and for notifying the airport, FBO, hangar operator, or facility in advance when required. Service does not imply guaranteed airport access or an airport/FBO affiliation.
